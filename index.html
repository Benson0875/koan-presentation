01 / LOOK BENEATH THE WINTHREE DAYS TO PAYDAY

WHAT ALEX DOES NOT SAY AT FIRST

The fear underneath.

Alex's guild has not said they will leave him out. He is afraid it could happen, so every new release feels urgent.

01

What we see

Cup noodles, an empty wallet, and a phone asking him to buy more. The scene looks funny until we hear why he keeps checking.

02

What he fears

“I don't want to be the useless one.” His real worry is losing his place with other players.

03

What he has lost

He used to look forward to playing. Now he spends more time looking at what he is missing.

Our reading: he is hungry for belonging as much as for the next character.

01 / WHAT WE THINKONE WHO IS NEVER HUNGRY

OUR READING

What feeds the fear?

We connect the koan's “never full” image to the fear beneath Alex's spending.

01

The game can still be fun.

Alex does not need to quit playing. He says he wants the game to feel fun again.

02

Fear keeps asking for more.

Each new character promises relief, but his worry about being left out soon returns.

03

He makes one small choice.

Alex puts his phone down for one meal. He has not solved everything, but he has found a place to begin.

Our question: “What are we feeding when we never feel full?”

01 / A SMALL CHANGETHREE DAYS TO PAYDAY

BACK TO THE FILM

One meal without checking.

Jamie also plays the game. He enjoys free characters and casual matches. He helps Alex notice that playing and chasing every release are different choices.

01

Jamie does not decide for him.

When Alex asks if he should stop playing, Jamie asks, “Do you want to?”

02

Alex names one limit.

He turns his phone face down and chooses to eat this meal without checking the game.

03

The ending stays open.

Alex may still feel pressure tomorrow. For now, he can enjoy lunch and a casual match with Jamie after work.

The film offers a starting point, not a sudden cure for wanting more.

02 / A CHANGE IN VIEWSOME WERE NOT

NORA'S TURN

Who belongs in this room?

Nora first judges which students have a medical reason to be there. Dr. Lee asks her to find out what she has not heard.

01

Her first view

Sam has a fever. Evan lied. Mia is upset about a grade. Nora puts them into separate boxes too quickly.

02

Dr. Lee's answer

“Fear isn't a fever. But a thermometer can't tell us everything.” He keeps the differences clear and listens to each student.

03

Nora notices the cost

She says she was so busy deciding who belonged that she stopped listening.

The question changes from “Which label fits?” to “What does this person need?”

02 / WHAT WE THINKONE WHO IS NOT SICK

OUR READING

Care starts with listening.

Dr. Lee says, “Fear isn't a fever.” Still, “sick” and “not sick” do not tell Nora everything about a person.

01

A fever needs care.

Sam's physical illness is real. Dr. Lee makes sure he is safe and comfortable.

02

A lie still needs an honest answer.

Evan made up his stomachache. He also fears failure and laughter. Nora can address both.

03

A grade is not an identity.

Mia's result hurts. It does not decide who she is. Nora offers to stay while they ask a counselor for help.

Nora's answer: “Some were sick. Some weren't. All needed care.”

02 / CARE IN ACTIONSOME WERE NOT

BACK TO THE FILM

Listening leads to action.

The ending does not solve every problem. It shows a different next step for each student.

01

Sam gets medical care.

Dr. Lee keeps him comfortable and contacts his family. His fever remains a real health concern.

02

Evan tells the truth.

Nora offers to speak with his teacher together. Support does not erase his responsibility for the lie.

03

Mia is not left alone.

Nora offers to stay while they ask a counselor to join them. A bad grade is no longer the only thing being seen.

Care is more than a feeling here. It becomes an honest, practical next step.

WHAT EACH KOAN ADDSTWO DISTINCT STORIES

BEYOND THE COMPARISON

Two koans. Different lessons.

Both films ask us to look again, but they explore different problems.

01

Hunger and “enough”

Alex keeps reaching for another game character. Our film explores how fear can make “enough” move away. The koan's other figure, who never eats and is never hungry, remains open to more thought.

02

Sickness and a person

Nora learns that “sick” and “not sick” cannot tell her all she needs to know. The film keeps a fever, a lie, and a painful grade distinct.

03

Our shared question

Before we decide what a moment means, can we pause long enough to notice what we missed?
