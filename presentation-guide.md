# Group 2 rehearsal guide

This guide is for the group. The website does not display speaker names or time allocations.

For a complete page-by-page English rehearsal draft, use the [full speaking script](full-speaking-script.md). The short cues below are for a quick review before presenting.

The 16-page deck is paced for about **17½ minutes of speaking**, plus the two planned **2½-minute films**. Rehearse aloud: questions, audience responses, and the final film lengths will change the total.

| Presenter | Pages | Speaking target | Film |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 1–4: opening, first koan, first film, turning point | 3½ min | 2½ min |
| 2 | 5–7: Alex's fear, our reading, his small choice | 3½ min | — |
| 3 | 8–10: second koan, second film, three students | 2¾ min | 2½ min |

Presenters 1 and 3 also cue the films, so they have less speaking than Presenter 5. Use one sentence for each handoff; the next person should continue the idea instead of starting a new introduction. Assign real names to Presenter 1–5 during rehearsal. Speaker names and timing stay off the website.

## Page cues

1. **Cover · 45 sec.** Name both koans. Explain the sequence: original passage, everyday story, then the group's reading.
2. **First koan · 75 sec.** Read the full course-handout passage on the slide. Ask why the person who eats all day might still feel empty. Leave the “never hungry” figure open.
3. **Film 1 · 30 sec speaking + film.** Introduce Alex, Jamie, and the three days before payday. Ask the audience to notice what Alex fears beneath the purchases.
4. **A win feels empty · 60 sec.** Compare Alex's stated goal with his fear of being excluded. Use the two lines on the page.
5. **The fear underneath · 75 sec.** Move from the visible joke about noodles to Alex's fear of being the “useless one.” Explain how checking replaces enjoying the game.
6. **Our reading · 75 sec.** Connect “never full” to a goal that keeps moving. Make clear that the film explores one reading of the koan.
7. **One meal · 60 sec.** Explain Jamie's question and Alex's small choice. His life has not been solved in one lunch.
8. **Second koan · 75 sec.** Read the course-handout excerpt on the slide. It ends with “Some were sick, some were not.” Ask what the labels “sick” and “not sick” tell us, and what they leave out.
9. **Film 2 · 30 sec speaking + film.** Introduce the school clinic. Ask the audience to watch when Nora starts listening differently.
10. **Three students · 60 sec.** Separate Sam's fever, Evan's lie and fear, and Mia's distress after the grade. Their needs are different.
11. **Who belongs? · 75 sec.** Contrast Nora's first sorting with Dr. Lee's question about what has not been heard.
12. **Our reading · 75 sec.** Explain that fear is not a fever. Show how care, honesty, and support can each matter without turning every emotion into a diagnosis.
13. **Care in action · 60 sec.** Point to each student's practical next step. The film ends with care beginning, not with every problem resolved.
14. **Comparison · 90 sec.** Read across each row: what is seen, what is missed, and what changes. Invite one quick observation from the room.
15. **Different lessons · 75 sec.** Keep the koans distinct: the first explores wanting and “enough”; the second tests what a label can tell us about a person.
16. **Final question · 90 sec.** Pause after asking whether we can see a person beyond the first label. Let one or two people respond, then close.

The revised films are embedded on pages 3 and 9. Start each one with its video controls, and advance only after playback ends.

## What each presenter should say

These are short English speaking cues. Use the slide to expand each point naturally; you do not need to memorize full paragraphs.

### Presenter 1 · Pages 1–4

- **Page 1:** “We are Group 2. We will read two koans, show an everyday story for each one, and share our own reading.” Name both titles. Say that the films are possible interpretations, not final answers.
- **Page 2:** Read the passage aloud. Then say: “The two people make us ask what hunger and fullness could mean beyond food. We focus first on the person who keeps eating but never feels full.” Pause briefly after “The assembly was silent.”
- **Page 3:** “In *Three Days to Payday*, Alex has spent so much on a game that he is eating cup noodles before payday. Watch what he wants after he gets the character he was waiting for.” Cue Film 1.
- **Page 4:** “Alex says he needs thirty more pulls, then he will be done. Jamie asks where the finish line is. The answer changes when Alex admits he is afraid his guild will not choose him.” Point to the two lines on screen.
- **Pass to Presenter 2:** “So what is Alex really trying to get when he keeps spending?”

### Presenter 2 · Pages 5–7

- **Page 5:** “Alex's guild has not actually said they will leave him out. He is afraid they might. His words, ‘the useless one,’ show that he worries about belonging.” Explain how he now checks what he is missing instead of enjoying the game.
- **Page 6:** “We connect Alex with the person who eats all day but never feels full. A new character gives short relief, but the fear returns. Our film explores one reading of the koan; it does not say that games or spending are always wrong.” Ask: “What are we feeding when we never feel full?”
- **Page 7:** “Jamie also plays. He does not tell Alex to quit. Alex chooses one meal without checking his phone. That is a small beginning, not a complete cure.” End on the casual match after work.
- **Pass to Presenter 3:** “The next koan also asks us to look again, this time at how we see other people.”

### Presenter 3 · Pages 8–10

- **Page 8:** Read the entire excerpt shown on screen. Then say: “Guishan asks how many people were sick. Daowu sorts them into two groups. We wondered what these two labels can tell us, and what they might leave out.” Do not add later lines from another English version.
- **Page 9:** “In *Some Were Not*, Nora meets three students in a school clinic. One has a fever, one lied to avoid a test, and one is overwhelmed by a bad grade. Watch how Nora's first judgment changes.” Cue Film 2.
- **Page 10:** “Sam needs medical care. Evan needs to tell the truth, but he is also afraid. Mia needs someone to listen. The three needs are different; the words ‘sick’ and ‘not sick’ do not explain all of them.”
- **Pass to Presenter 4:** “What changes when Nora stops sorting and starts listening?”

### Presenter 4 · Pages 11–13

- **Page 11:** “At first, Nora thinks about who belongs in the clinic. Dr. Lee reminds her that a thermometer cannot tell us everything. Nora then notices that she was so busy deciding who belonged that she stopped listening.”
- **Page 12:** “Our reading is that a label can be useful without being a full description of a person. Sam's fever is real. Evan's lie is real too. Mia's pain also matters. ‘All needed care’ is our film's response, not a line from the original excerpt.”
- **Page 13:** “Care becomes practical: Sam rests and his family is contacted; Evan is encouraged to face his teacher honestly; Mia is offered support from a counselor. The ending begins to help them; it does not solve everything.”
- **Pass to Presenter 5:** “Now we can put the two films side by side.”

### Presenter 5 · Pages 14–16

- **Page 14:** Read the comparison row by row: what we see first, what lies underneath, and what changes. “Alex fears being left behind. Evan fears being laughed at. Mia feels defined by a grade. Jamie and Dr. Lee both ask questions that reveal more.” Invite one short observation if there is time.
- **Page 15:** “The first koan raises a question about wanting and enough. The second raises a question about what labels can tell us about a person. Our films focus on part of each passage, so we leave other possible meanings open.” Mention that the first koan's person who never eats is not fully explained by Alex's story.
- **Page 16:** “The first thing we see may be true, but it may not be the whole story. Alex keeps spending. Evan did lie. Sam really has a fever. What changes when we ask what each person is dealing with?” Ask the room: “Can we see the person beyond the label?” Take one or two brief responses, then say, “Thank you for listening.”

Rehearse once with a timer. The target is **15–20 minutes of speaking**, with the planned **5 minutes of films counted separately**.
